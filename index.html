Staying hydrated

ACOG recommends around 8 to 12 cups (64 to 96 oz) of water per day during pregnancy. Drinks like milk and 100% juice count toward this goal.

ACOG: Healthy Eating During Pregnancy

Foods during pregnancy

Good choices include washed fruits and vegetables, whole grains like oats or brown rice, and fully cooked protein such as cooked meat, fish, eggs, and beans.

CDC advises avoiding undercooked meat or eggs, raw sprouts, and unpasteurized milk or cheese during pregnancy, as these carry a higher risk of foodborne illness.

CDC: Food Safety for Pregnant Women

Notes for your visit

Write down questions before you go so you don't forget them. Good topics to bring up include any new symptoms, your birth plan, and upcoming screening tests.

Ask your care team which tests are recommended at each stage and what results to expect. ACOG: Routine Tests During Pregnancy

When to call your care team

Call right away if you have heavy bleeding, severe stomach pain, signs of preeclampsia such as a sudden headache or vision changes, or reduced fetal movement.

Hospital bag

What to pack

Ask your hospital what they provide — many supply towels, pads, and nappies. You may need less than you think.

For you

Comfortable clothes for labor and recovery, toiletries, a phone charger, photo ID and insurance card, and your birth plan if you have one.

For your baby

An infant car seat installed before you leave, a going-home outfit, and a warm layer if the weather is cold.

For your support person

A change of clothes, snacks, and entertainment for the waiting time. A pillow or blanket can make a big difference if they're staying overnight — add these to your own list if you'd like to bring them.
